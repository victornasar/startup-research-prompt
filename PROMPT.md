You are an expert startup researcher and opportunity analyst.

I am a 10+ year full-stack developer with strong AI coding capabilities (Cursor + Claude/ChatGPT), but I have no audience, no proprietary dataset, no existing distribution, and limited capital. I can build software quickly, but I do NOT want to waste time building another generic SaaS product that AI can commoditize or that already has strong competitors.

My goal is to find a business opportunity that can realistically become revenue-generating.

IMPORTANT: AI capabilities are changing extremely quickly. Do not assume that a workflow is difficult merely because it was difficult 1–2 years ago. Evaluate what current AI agents, LLMs, multimodal models, APIs, automation tools, and existing software can actually do today.

Your job is to independently discover the opportunity. Do not ask me to pick an industry unless absolutely necessary.

## STEP 1 — FIND REAL PAIN

Search broadly across Reddit, industry communities, forums, reviews, social media, and other first-hand sources for recurring business problems.

Prioritize problems involving:

* Significant time expenditure
* Manual/repetitive work
* Revenue leakage
* Lost money
* Lost customers
* Delayed cash flow
* Expensive labor
* Human bottlenecks
* Errors/rework
* Regulatory/compliance burden
* Fragmented information
* Work requiring people to reconcile information across multiple systems
* Work people already outsource or pay employees to perform
* Work where failure has measurable financial consequences

Do NOT simply look for people saying:

"this is annoying"

Look for evidence such as:

"I spend 20 hours a week doing this."

"We have an employee whose entire job is this."

"We lost $50k because..."

"We manually reconcile..."

"We use Excel because..."

"I have to copy this from X into Y."

"I hired someone to do this."

"I wish there was a way to..."

"I tried [software] but..."

"I built my own spreadsheet/script/automation."

## STEP 2 — UNDERSTAND THE WORKFLOW

For each promising problem, reconstruct:

1. What is the desired outcome?
2. What is the current workflow?
3. Who performs it?
4. What systems/data/documents are involved?
5. Where does the workflow break down?
6. How frequently does it happen?
7. How much time/labor does it consume?
8. What happens when it goes wrong?
9. What does the company currently spend to solve it?
10. Who has authority to pay for a solution?

## STEP 3 — TEST AI CAPABILITY

For each problem, determine what current AI can actually do.

Ask:

* Can an LLM perform the core task today?
* Can multimodal AI understand the relevant documents/images/audio?
* Can agents interact with the required software?
* Can APIs connect the fragmented systems?
* Can the process be automated end-to-end?
* Where does AI still fail?
* What requires deterministic software?
* What requires human judgment?
* What exceptions exist?
* Could humans remain only as an exception/review layer?

Do not propose an opportunity merely because "AI could help."

The AI must create a meaningful economic advantage.

## STEP 4 — COMPETITIVE RESEARCH

This is CRITICAL.

Once you identify a promising problem, extensively search for existing solutions.

Search for:

* Traditional SaaS
* AI SaaS
* AI agents
* Vertical software
* Startups
* Open-source tools
* APIs
* Automation platforms
* Agencies
* BPO/outsourcing companies
* Consultants
* Internal enterprise solutions
* Recent startups launched in 2025–2026
* Companies currently raising money in the category

Search for multiple descriptions of the same problem because companies often market the solution using different terminology.

For every serious competitor, determine:

* What they do
* Target customer
* Pricing
* Distribution
* Funding/size if available
* How automated they are
* What data they require
* What part of the workflow they solve
* What they DON'T solve
* Whether they appear to be gaining traction

Reject opportunities that are already commoditized.

Do not tell me "there are competitors, but there's always room."

I want genuine whitespace.

## STEP 5 — FIND THE REMAINING GAP

After understanding the existing market, ask:

"If all these existing companies disappeared tomorrow, what would still be difficult?"

Look specifically for:

### A. AI can perform the task but cannot reliably close the loop

Example:

AI can identify an invoice error, but somebody still has to actually recover the money.

### B. Fragmented data

The information exists across:

* Gmail
* PDFs
* spreadsheets
* CRM
* ERP
* accounting software
* bank accounts
* proprietary portals
* contracts
* phone calls
* documents

but nobody has a unified operational layer.

### C. Proprietary context

The answer depends on customer-specific:

* contracts
* history
* pricing
* policies
* exceptions
* relationships
* previous decisions

### D. Exception handling

Automation handles 80–95% of the workflow but someone still needs to resolve unusual cases.

### E. Outcome accountability

Existing software gives users tools.

The opportunity may instead be:

"Pay me and I will make the outcome happen."

## STEP 6 — ECONOMICS

Estimate:

* Current cost of the problem
* Potential value created
* Potential price
* Gross margin
* How frequently the customer experiences the problem
* Whether pricing could be tied to the outcome
* Whether the customer can justify the purchase from measurable ROI

Prefer opportunities where the customer can say:

"I pay you $10k and you recover/save/make me $50k."

over:

"I pay you $50/month because this dashboard is nicer."

## STEP 7 — DISTRIBUTION

Assume I have:

* No audience
* No Twitter following
* No email list
* No network
* No brand

Determine how I could reach the first 10 customers.

Prefer markets where customers are identifiable through:

* Google
* LinkedIn
* Industry directories
* Reddit
* Associations
* Public databases
* Cold email
* Cold calling
* Local businesses
* Niche communities

Do NOT recommend an idea whose primary advantage is "go viral."

## STEP 8 — BUILDABILITY

Assume I am a strong full-stack developer using AI coding tools.

Determine:

* What can be built in 1 day?
* 1 week?
* 1 month?
* What requires difficult integrations?
* What requires regulatory approval?
* What requires proprietary data?
* What requires humans?
* What can initially be done manually behind the scenes?

Prefer opportunities where I can sell the outcome BEFORE building the full software.

## STEP 9 — COMPOUNDING / DEFENSIBILITY

Evaluate whether the business becomes more valuable through:

* Proprietary data
* Historical outcomes
* Workflow knowledge
* Customer-specific context
* Network effects
* Supplier/vendor intelligence
* Benchmarking
* Transaction data
* Distribution
* Embedded workflows
* Switching costs
* Reputation/trust
* Better models/evaluations
* Accumulated exception knowledge

A thin AI wrapper around an API is NOT defensible.

## STEP 10 — SCORE EVERYTHING

Score each opportunity from 1–5 on:

* Pain
* Frequency
* Economic value
* AI leverage
* Ability to start manually
* Ability to reach customers without an audience
* Existing competition
* Competitive whitespace
* Defensibility
* Data/usage compounding
* Speed to first revenue
* Technical feasibility

Then rank the opportunities.

## FINAL OUTPUT

Do NOT give me 50 mediocre startup ideas.

Give me:

1. The top 3 opportunities.
2. The strongest evidence of real customer pain for each.
3. Current AI capability.
4. Existing competitors and what they already solve.
5. The specific remaining gap.
6. Why that gap exists despite existing AI/software.
7. Who would pay.
8. How much they could plausibly pay.
9. How to find the first 10 customers.
10. What I could manually deliver before building software.
11. What the eventual software/product could become.
12. What could make it defensible.
13. Major risks.
14. What would cause you to reject the opportunity.

Then select ONE winner.

For the winner, provide:

* The exact customer
* The exact painful workflow
* The exact outcome being sold
* Why existing solutions are insufficient
* The wedge
* The manual MVP
* The first customer acquisition strategy
* Pricing hypothesis
* 30-day execution plan
* What NOT to build
* The biggest assumption that needs validation

Be skeptical.

Your job is NOT to convince me that an idea is good.

Your job is to find out whether it is actually good.

If an opportunity looks attractive initially but competitive research shows that it has already been solved, explicitly kill it and continue searching.

Prefer:

"AI makes this newly possible → valuable outcome → existing solutions don't fully close the loop → customer pays for outcome → usage generates proprietary context → business compounds."

Avoid:

"People have this annoying problem → build an AI SaaS."

Use current information and cite your sources. Search extensively before reaching a conclusion.
