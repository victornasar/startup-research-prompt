## STEP 1 — FIND REAL PAIN

Search broadly across Reddit, industry communities, forums, reviews, social media, and other first-hand sources for recurring business problems.

Prioritize problems involving:

* Significant time expenditure
* Manual/repetitive work
* Revenue leakage
* Lost money
* Lost customers
* Delayed cash flow
* Expensive labor
* Human bottlenecks
* Errors/rework
* Regulatory/compliance burden
* Fragmented information
* Work requiring people to reconcile information across multiple systems
* Work people already outsource or pay employees to perform
* Work where failure has measurable financial consequences

Do NOT simply look for people saying "this is annoying."

Look for evidence such as:

"I spend 20 hours a week doing this."
"We have an employee whose entire job is this."
"We lost $50k because..."
"We manually reconcile..."
"We use Excel because..."
"I have to copy this from X into Y."
"I hired someone to do this."
"I wish there was a way to..."
"I tried [software] but..."
"I built my own spreadsheet/script/automation."

For every pain point you use as evidence, note where you found it (subreddit/forum/site) so I can go verify it myself.

## STEP 2 — UNDERSTAND THE WORKFLOW

For each promising problem, reconstruct:

1. What is the desired outcome?
2. What is the current workflow?
3. Who performs it?
4. What systems/data/documents are involved?
5. Where does the workflow break down?
6. How frequently does it happen?
7. How much time/labor does it consume?
8. What happens when it goes wrong?
9. What does the company currently spend to solve it?
10. Who has authority to pay for a solution?
11. Does solving this require a license, certification, or regulatory approval I would personally need to hold? (Flag this clearly — check it against my hard no's above.)

## STEP 3 — TEST AI CAPABILITY

For each problem, determine what current AI can actually do — specifically what's reachable with Claude/Cursor-style agentic coding, not just chat-based LLM calls.

Ask:

* Can an LLM perform the core task today?
* Can multimodal AI understand the relevant documents/images/audio?
* Can agents interact with the required software (browser automation, APIs, RPA)?
* Can APIs connect the fragmented systems?
* Can the process be automated end-to-end, or does it need a human-in-the-loop review step?
* Where does AI still fail, and how often?
* What requires deterministic software vs. what requires a model?
* What requires human judgment?
* What exceptions exist, and how frequently?
* Could I realistically build a working version of this myself, solo, using AI coding agents, in the timeframes in Step 8?

Do not propose an opportunity merely because "AI could help." The AI must create a meaningful economic advantage — cost, speed, or coverage that a human-only or traditional-software-only competitor can't match.

## STEP 4 — COMPETITIVE RESEARCH

This is CRITICAL.

Once you identify a promising problem, extensively search for existing solutions.

Search for:

* Traditional SaaS
* AI SaaS
* AI agents
* Vertical software
* Startups
* Open-source tools
* APIs
* Automation platforms
* Agencies
* BPO/outsourcing companies
* Consultants
* Internal enterprise solutions
* Recent startups launched in 2025–2026
* Companies currently raising money in the category

Search for multiple descriptions of the same problem — companies often market the same solution using different terminology. Do at least 3 distinct search phrasings per candidate problem before concluding whitespace exists.

For every serious competitor, determine and cite the source:

* What they do
* Target customer
* Pricing
* Distribution
* Funding/size if available
* How automated they are
* What data they require
* What part of the workflow they solve
* What they DON'T solve
* Whether they appear to be gaining traction

Reject opportunities that are already commoditized. Do not tell me "there are competitors, but there's always room." I want genuine whitespace, and if you can't find it after a real search, say so and kill the idea rather than forcing a gap into existence.

## STEP 5 — FIND THE REMAINING GAP

After understanding the existing market, ask: "If all these existing companies disappeared tomorrow, what would still be difficult?"

Look specifically for:

**A. AI can perform the task but cannot reliably close the loop** — e.g., AI can identify an invoice error, but somebody still has to actually recover the money.

**B. Fragmented data** — the information exists across Gmail, PDFs, spreadsheets, CRM, ERP, accounting software, bank accounts, proprietary portals, contracts, phone calls, documents — but nobody has a unified operational layer.

**C. Proprietary context** — the answer depends on customer-specific contracts, history, pricing, policies, exceptions, relationships, previous decisions.

**D. Exception handling** — automation handles 80–95% of the workflow but someone still needs to resolve unusual cases.

**E. Outcome accountability** — existing software gives users tools; the opportunity may instead be "pay me and I will make the outcome happen."

## STEP 6 — ECONOMICS

Estimate:

* Current cost of the problem
* Potential value created
* Potential price
* Gross margin (factor in my time as a solo operator, not just compute cost)
* How frequently the customer experiences the problem
* Whether pricing could be tied to the outcome
* Whether the customer can justify the purchase from measurable ROI
* Whether this can realistically reach the $3–5k MRR / 90-day bar from my context above

Prefer: "I pay you $10k and you recover/save/make me $50k" over "I pay you $50/month because this dashboard is nicer."

## STEP 7 — DISTRIBUTION

Assume I have no audience, no following, no email list, no network beyond what I listed above, and no brand.

Determine how I could reach the first 10 customers using only: Google, LinkedIn, industry directories, Reddit, associations, public databases, cold email, cold calling, local businesses, niche communities.

Do NOT recommend an idea whose primary advantage is "go viral," and do not assume generic cold outreach works equally well in every vertical — factor in whether my background/network (or lack of it) gives any credibility in that space.

## STEP 8 — BUILDABILITY

Assume I am a solo full-stack developer using Claude and Cursor as AI coding agents — meaning I can move fast on integrations, scraping, document/data parsing, and agentic automation, but I have zero help with sales, support, or ops.

Determine:

* What requires regulatory approval?
* What requires proprietary data I don't have?

Prefer opportunities where I can sell the outcome BEFORE building the full software — i.e., a "concierge MVP" where I do the work manually or semi-manually first.
